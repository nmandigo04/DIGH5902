I researched one of the projects from Reviews in Digital Humanities titled “This Was Paris in 1970” and found it pretty interesting. It was a project about 100,000 photos that had been compiled from a photography competition in Paris in 1970 in which 15,000 photographers captured the cityscape before rapid industrial change.

Centering on the questions of “How do pictures influence the production of new forms of knowledge? What is their role in representing the world?”, this project used neural networks, Bayesian networks, and other networks to analyze patterns across the data set. It features information on topics such as Parisian urban layouts, urban life, and even fashion trends - with the central theme of demonstrating the cultural and economic changes happening in Paris in the 1960s and 1970s.

While this was all information from the review page, I found the actual website quite difficult to navigate and impossible to find the actual information and photographs this project complied. Maybe it’s been discontinued,  is just full of bugs, or I’m terrible at navigating this site.

However, the premise of this project is intriguing for me, and I would be interested to learn more about how this approach benefited scholars in contextualizing this point in history, particularly the role that images played in doing so and what the end-products of this project looked like as an educational tool.
