Week 2 I did this:
In preparation for class this day, I did the readings (while taking notes in Obsidian), with the piece from _Current Research in Digital History_ that I read being a project by Anna McGee titled _Visualizing Art on the Threshold in the Palazzo Medici, Florence_ from 2025.
I chose this because of it's relationship to Art History, and usage of the digital humanities in a gallery setting. To summarize, this was a project that made a digital version of the Palazzo Medici using the software _Autodesk Maya_ to replicate Filippo Lippi's c.1450 _Annunciation_ painting. This was done in collaboration with the London National Gallery, with the purpose of demonstrating the importance of the relationship between the painting and where it was positioned in the dwelling. McGee proposes the potential uses and advantages of making 3D digital models as scholarly tools and gallery experiences for visitors. 
With the guiding questions from the class, and after doing the other readings for the week, what I mainly took away was how inaccessible this project was for the greater public. While it was used for the gallery, it totally could have been made available to any one interested. However, the bureaucracies of galleries remain.
