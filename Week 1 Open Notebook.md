Week 1 I did this:
As this was the first week of DIGH 5902, or any Digital Humanities course for that matter, I found it to be a good introduction to the field in the sense that it didn't exactly have a clear definition. It was clear that this class would test me, and push me to think about new ways of using technology in the humanities in ways I hadn't considered prior. What stood out the most to me and what I took away from this first class was this unique mindset that the Digital Humanities has towards academia and historical research. Such mindsets are as follows:
  Digital history is not done by lone, heroic scholars, but by many humans collaborating to produce something that can be used by other humans. That this sense of asking for help, and seeking the perspectives and knowledge of others is a sign of progress, not failure.
  Coding (and while I've never written a line of code) is not about finding the answer, but going through the process of dealing with failures. This will test my patience and learning skills, as it will feel like learning a new language.
  Pursing something that interests me and developing the foundational abilities for pursing those interests will be directly tied to the goals of this course. As I grapple with my masters research, I am open to the wide array of digital tools that I can apply, so long as they bring value and not just "oh this is cool to know, but not very helpful in formulating an argument"
